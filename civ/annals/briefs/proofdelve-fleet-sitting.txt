================================================================================
0. WHY THIS SITTING EXISTS
================================================================================

The Overseer asked whether the civilization construct is paying for itself. It
was measured on 2026-09-02 from the forts' own event streams, git, and bd,
across fortkit, ForgeOs, longburn and WWWW over 2026-08-03 to 09-02. The
findings that drive this sitting:

  - Forge sessions: 167 paired in the capital, MEDIAN 3.8 MINUTES, p90 7.0,
    longest ever 14.6. Same shape in all three active forts (Proofdelve median
    4.3, longburn 3.7). Total Forge wall-clock across the whole civilization
    for the month: 43.1 hours. Total Warden wall-clock: 64.5 hours.
    THE CIVILIZATION SPENDS 1.5x MORE TIME REVIEWING WORK THAN DOING IT.

  - Activity by hour, three forts, 31 days: 03:00-06:00 is EXACTLY ZERO, every
    day. The overnight capability has never once been exercised.

  - 325 beads ready, 2 in progress. `assignee` is set on 2 of 355 beads.
    Atomic claiming exists in the tool and is decorative: forge.sh claims a
    bead AFTER the Mayor has already chosen it by hand.

  - There is no dispatcher, no queue consumer, no fleet. `forge.sh <bead-id>`
    takes one bead and is invoked by hand. The architecture has producers and
    no consumers.

The diagnosis: the governance layer was built to an unusual standard and the
production layer was never built at all. This sitting builds the production
layer, in the fort where the product work actually is.

P1 — HONEST READY QUEUE: migrate human and Regent gates to `bd gate`
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: `bd ready` currently offers beads that are blocked on the Overseer. A
naive dispatcher would hand a Regent-lane bead to a Forge that cannot possibly
do it. `bd gate create --blocks <id>` creates a real blocking issue and the
blocked bead LEAVES `bd ready` outright. Our `gate-N` labels do not.

SCOPE MEASUREMENT WAS TAKEN IN THE CAPITAL, NOT IN PROOFDELVE. In fortkit:
65 open beads carry a human/Regent gate label, and 21 OF THOSE ARE STILL
OFFERED BY `bd ready` AS WORKABLE. RE-MEASURE IN PROOFDELVE BEFORE ACTING.
The gate-label convention was a Manyhalls adoption (fortkit-fci.1, 2026-08-08)
and may not have propagated. If Proofdelve labels differently, migrate what is
actually there, not what this brief predicts. Do not report the capital's
number as Proofdelve's.

DO:
  - Measure Proofdelve's actual gate labelling and the label/ready overlap.
  - For each open bead genuinely blocked on the Overseer or on a Regent lane,
    create a `bd gate` of type `human` with a reason naming what is awaited.
  - Keep the labels in place for one cycle as a cross-check. Do not delete
    them in this sitting.

[ASK] APPROVAL POINT A — CHARTER AMENDMENT. The gate-label convention is
written into Proofdelve's law. Changing the mechanism is a constitutional
edit, prose-gated for attended seats: the Overseer's approval must be recorded
on the bead BEFORE the edit, and `charter.amended` must be emitted. Ask him in
the moment. If he declines, P1 stops and P2 must then carry its own
gate-exclusion filter, which is worse but workable. Say so if it happens.

ACCEPTANCE:
  - `bd ready` in Proofdelve returns zero beads that are blocked on a human or
    on the Regent lane. Verified by re-running the overlap measurement and
    getting 0.
  - Every gate created names what it waits for in its reason string.
  - `charter.amended` emitted, approval recorded on the bead beforehand.

P2 — THE DISPATCHER: the consumer side
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: this is the missing half of the architecture. Deterministic, no model, no
tokens. Crons watch, models act.

DO:
  - A loop that pulls `bd ready` filtered to an OPT-IN label (propose
    `fleet-safe`; let Marrek own its semantics afterward). NO BEAD IS
    FLEET-SAFE UNTIL A MAYOR LABELS IT. Opt-in, never opt-out. This is what
    makes the first run small and deliberate.
  - Atomic claim via `bd update <id> --claim -a <proofdelve forge actor>`
    BEFORE launch, so a second dispatcher instance cannot double-book. Read
    the actor id from Proofdelve's own launchers.
  - Launch their forge.sh per claimed bead in its own worktree. Respect the
    existing flock; do not remove it. It guards a different concern (one
    launcher per worktree) from bd's claim (one worker per bead).
  - LAUNCH CONTEXT: the dispatcher writes outside the repo and spawns
    launchers, so it CANNOT be started from a masked seat. Same mechanism as
    herald.sh, measured 2026-08-06: seat-sandbox.sh binds $HOME read-only with
    only the repo rw and every spawned process inherits it. It must run from
    the Overseer's terminal or a systemd user timer. Install it the way H7 was
    installed.
  - FOLD IN, same file, same sitting: trim what forge.sh makes the Forge read
    at session start. It currently instructs a read of AGENTS.md, the charter,
    memory/current.md and the seat file before implementing. In the capital
    that is ~80KB, on the order of 20,000 tokens, at the head of a session
    whose median total length is 3.8 minutes. A Forge does not need the
    covenant or the full charter to implement one bead. Measure Proofdelve's
    equivalent sizes and cut to what the seat actually needs.

ACCEPTANCE:
  - Dispatcher run with 3 pre-labelled beads launches 3 Forges, each in its own
    worktree, each with the bead claimed to the Forge actor before launch.
  - Dispatcher run with 0 fleet-safe beads exits cleanly and quietly.
  - A bead already claimed or already in a live worktree is never double-launched.
  - Measured before/after byte count of the Forge's mandatory reading set,
    recorded on the bead.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P3 — THE PORTCULLIS: work lands while the Overseer sleeps
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: closure currently requires an attended Mayor session. That is the single
reason nothing can complete overnight.

DEPENDS ON P5. DO NOT ENABLE BEFORE P5 IS GREEN. See section 4.

DO:
  - On Forge exit 0 AND launcher-observed verifier green: route the bead to
    review. Note the known inversion (rvly in the capital: forge.sh has exited
    0 and printed the review recipe when its own observed verify exited 1).
    Check Proofdelve's forge.sh for the same shape and fail closed if found.
  - On Warden verdict PASS **and** verdict_recorded true: acquire the merge
    slot, merge, release, close the bead.
  - On BLOCK: return the bead to ready, append the verdict to the bead's
    COMMENTS (standing order 7: corrections go to comments, notes carry working
    state only), increment a retry counter, and after 2 returns escalate to the
    Mayor's queue instead of retrying. Never retry indefinitely.
  - FOLD IN: use `bd merge-slot` for merge serialization rather than inventing
    one. It is an exclusive primitive built for exactly this race, and we
    already solved this once by hand with flock after the 2026-08-04
    double-launch incident.

[ASK] APPROVAL POINT B — CLOSURE AUTHORITY. Proofdelve's forge.sh prompt says
"the Mayor closes it after review and merge". Automatic closure moves that
authority to a mechanism. Standing order 9 (no bead closes without verifiers
green plus review) is SATISFIED by this design, not weakened: what changes is
who presses the button. Get the Overseer's explicit yes, record it on the bead,
and emit `charter.amended` if their law names the Mayor as closer.

ACCEPTANCE:
  - A bead that passes verifier and review is merged and closed with no
    attended session, and the closing actor is identifiable in the record as
    the mechanism, not as a citizen.
  - A bead whose review returns BLOCK is returned to ready with the verdict in
    its comments, and is escalated rather than retried on the third return.
  - A bead whose review recorded NO verdict is NEVER closed. Fail closed.

P4 — THE BUDGET GOVERNOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: the Overseer runs 1 Claude Max ($200 tier) and 1 Codex Pro, total. The
Forge runs on Codex; the Warden and both Mayors run on Claude. An ungoverned
fleet can burn a month's plan in a night, and the Claude side is shared with
review and with every Mayor session.

DO NOT HARDCODE A CONCURRENCY NUMBER. Neither the Mayor nor this brief has a
reliable figure for sustainable concurrent sessions on either plan, and a
guessed constant would be a false measurement of exactly the class this fort
keeps filing beads about.

DO:
  - Forge concurrency: START AT 3, adaptive. Treat rate-limit responses as
    back-pressure and step down. Step back up only after a sustained clean
    period. LOG WHAT IT ACTUALLY SUSTAINED, so the ceiling is measured after
    one night instead of assumed.
  - Warden concurrency: 1. Fixed. The Claude plan funds review, Marrek, and
    Emrith simultaneously; review concurrency is the thing most likely to
    starve everything else.
  - A hard stop: maximum total launches per run, so a runaway cannot empty the
    plan before anyone wakes up. Propose 40; let the Overseer set it.

ACCEPTANCE:
  - Simulated rate-limit response causes a measured step-down, not a crash and
    not a hot retry loop.
  - Hard stop halts the run and emits an incident naming the cap it hit.
  - The sustained-ceiling log exists and is readable.

P5 — VERDICT RECORDING REPAIR  (HARD PRECONDITION FOR P3)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: across the three forts, 76 Warden sessions recorded NO verdict at all,
roughly 14% of all reviews. Today that costs a relaunch. Under P3 it would mean
the fort closes beads on reviews that never happened, silently, overnight. This
is the worst failure this programme could produce and it is already latent.

RELATED CAPITAL BEADS, for the shape only, not for porting: fortkit-5mw (a
complete verdict produced but the transcript file parsed empty), fortkit-7kzi
(researcher.sh has the same shape, and the bound can still be beaten by an
earlier verdict-line). Check whether Proofdelve's warden.sh carries the same
defects. It very likely does; the architecture is shared. Verify, do not assume.

DO:
  - A Warden session that produces no parseable verdict must emit an explicit
    incident AND leave the bead in a state that P3 refuses to close.
  - The verdict must be taken from a bounded, unambiguous position, not from
    trailing bytes. The capital repaired this once (iist) after trailing
    content in a transcript could override a recorded review gate; a later
    session found an injected instruction sitting in a posted transcript.
  - Absence of a verdict must be distinguishable in the record from a verdict
    of PASS. Today it is not, reliably.

ACCEPTANCE:
  - A Warden session killed mid-review produces an incident and no verdict, and
    P3 refuses that bead.
  - A transcript with content after the verdict line cannot change the recorded
    verdict. Prove it with a crafted fixture.
  - Re-measure the no-verdict rate after the fix and record the number.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P6 — BATCHED REVIEW   (DEFERRABLE — drop this first if the sitting runs long)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: review is 1.66 launches per bead, each paying the full session-start
context load, out of the shared Claude plan. Raising Forge throughput makes
review the choke point immediately.

DO:
  - One Warden session takes N landed beads and emits ONE VERDICT LINE PER
    BEAD. A block on one bead must not block the other N-1.
  - If per-bead verdicts inside a batch prove fiddly, STOP AND LEAVE IT. The
    loop works at 1:1 review, just more expensively. Do not risk P5's
    guarantees to save tokens.

ACCEPTANCE:
  - A batch of 3 where the middle bead blocks results in 2 merged and 1
    returned, with three distinguishable verdicts in the record.
