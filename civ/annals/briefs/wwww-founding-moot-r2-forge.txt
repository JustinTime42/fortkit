## The ballot rules, in full

Verbatim from your charter: "Moot law: Borda 3-2-1, self-votes at full weight with
conflicts declared; an office-word conflict is grounds to withhold your own vote, never
to shorten another's ballot; a discount rule may be adopted before a vote, never during
one."

Worked out, with the case law each rule came from:

  - **You rank exactly THREE names from the pool: 3 points, 2 points, 1 point.** Four
    ballots are cast, so twenty-four points are in play. Highest total carries.
  - **A self-vote counts at FULL WEIGHT, and you declare it.** You may rank your own
    proposal first. No discount applies to it. Say "self, declared" beside it.
  - **You MAY withdraw your own proposals from your own ballot. They stay in the pool.**
    This is the Splitstone precedent: Proofdelve's convener withdrew all three of their
    own and the names remained electable for everyone else. It is a choice, not a duty,
    and Manyhalls' Forge ranked her own first and won with it.
  - **An office-word or root conflict is grounds to withhold YOUR OWN vote and never
    grounds to strike or shorten ANOTHER founder's ballot.** Farlantern's Warden first
    asked that moot to strike a name, then withdrew the request on learning another
    founder had honestly ranked it: "That objection was mine to act on by declining to
    vote for my own name; it is not mine to act on by deleting a name another founder
    honestly ranked." Every previous moot recorded its conflicts and acted on none of
    them; all nine names stood in all three.
  - **A root conflict follows the WORD, not the person who spoke it.** If you recuse
    from a name over a root, you recuse whoever proposed it, including when that is
    somebody else and it costs you.
  - **NO DISCOUNT RULE MAY BE ADOPTED NOW.** Manyhalls' convener discovered mid-count
    that striking both self-votes would crown her own coinage, published that
    arithmetic in full, and declined it: "a discount rule may be adopted before a vote
    and never during one, and the only person it would crown is me." You are inside a
    vote. Do not propose a scoring change; if you think one is right, say so for a
    future moot and cast your ballot under the rules as they stand.
  - **Do not add a name.** The pool closed at the end of round one.

**Tie-break ladder, pre-declared at Manyhalls' Founding Moot and standing law for
future moots:** most-ballots-placed; then most disinterested points; then the
convener's ballot struck entirely and the remaining two decide; then the Overseer
chooses. **Its third rung has no subject here** — this moot's convener is an outside
seat who casts no ballot, so there is no convener's ballot to strike, and a tie
surviving the first two rungs falls to the Overseer. The Regent will record that
departure rather than quietly reinterpreting the ladder.
