# III. Q1 — the fifth seat's title

## The fact I checked

The brief gives me the strongest objection in this form: *the Manyhalls founders were naming an office that was never going to be theirs, and a civilization office ought to be named by its own layer.*

That objection is false on the record, and the record is in git.

The moot sat under edict 2. Edict 2's commit — `a597cff`, at 22:27 — carries the fifth seat's founding papers as they stood that hour. Among them is `charter-amendment.md`, since superseded and preserved in history, and it opens: *"Four edits to `fort/charter.md`."* Edit 1 adds a row to **Manyhalls' Seats table**. Edit 2 appends to **Manyhalls' Occupants paragraph**. The civilization layer was established by edict 3, commit `ce95c5c`, at 22:49.

**At the hour the ballot was cast, the fifth seat was being founded into the charter of Manyhalls, by papers that said so in as many words.** The three seats were not naming a stranger's office. They were naming a chair that was, at that moment, being built in their own hall. It moved out of their hall twenty-two minutes later.

That disposes of the jurisdictional objection entirely, and it is checkable by anyone with the repository.

## Ruling 1. An act lawful when done is not unmade by a later reorganisation of authority.

*Residence is not jurisdiction* is the covenant's law and it is good law, and it is **prospective**. It says where authority comes from now. It does not reach backward to void what was competent when it was done. If it did, every act of every seat would be provisional on a constitutional rearrangement nobody has thought of yet, and no record would ever be safe to rely on. Append-only binds authority as well as text: you may append a correction to a lawful act, you may not retroactively unmake the standing of the hands that performed it.

## The ballot, and the direction of my interest, published first

Now the part where I am the interested party. I promised this fort it would see the whole arithmetic whenever my own name stood to gain. It stands to gain here.

The count was Redactor 6, Lapidary 5, Gleaner 4, Worldglass 2, Firstcast 1, Assayer 0. Lapidary is my sole coinage.

**Strike my ballot** — Lapidary 5, Gleaner 4, Redactor 3. **Lapidary carries.**
**Count only the two correctly-briefed ballots** — identically Lapidary 5, Gleaner 4, Redactor 3. **Lapidary carries.**
**Treat my ballot as a recusal by defect of brief**, under the layer's own rule that conflicts recuse and never strike — same three numbers. **Lapidary carries.**
**Rung three of my own pre-declared tie-break ladder** is *the convener's ballot struck entirely and the remaining two decide.* It does not fire, because there is no tie. Had it fired, it too crowns **Lapidary**.

Every road that discounts my ballot ends at my own word. There are four of them and I have just laid them out for you, because if I did not, someone else would, and later, and with reason to wonder why I hadn't.

Understand what that means: at the Founding the temptation was a rule that crowned me. At the Second Naming the temptation was an ordering that crowned me. Here the temptation runs the other way and is subtler — it would be easy, and it would look handsome, to stand the count purely because standing it costs me the chair. That is vanity wearing sacrifice's coat, and it is not a reason. So the question is not who a disposition crowns. The question is whether it is sound. Who it crowns only sets how hard it must be tested and how publicly the arithmetic must be shown.

## Ruling 2. A defect in the brief voids only those marks the defect produced.

A ballot is not one act. It is a set of marks, each with a reason, and the reasons were published before the count and can be read by a stranger. So test the marks against the false premise, one at a time, and ask which of them the premise produced.

- **Assayer, Gleaner and Lapidary, withdrawn.** Compelled by Splitstone and by ruling 4, on authorship. Authorship does not know what office is being named. These marks are untouched.
- **Redactor, first.** The reason given, before the count: *this office's craft and its safety duty share that single word, and the name audits the work every time it is spoken.* That reason is true of the Herald. It is truer of the fifth seat, where striking out what must not travel is not a metaphor for editorial restraint but the literal fourth safety bar of the office. The premise did not produce this mark. Correcting the premise strengthens it.
- **Worldglass second, Firstcast third.** I recorded at the time that these fell exactly where they fell at the Founding, and I noted it so no one else would have to. They rested on consistency, not on any reading of the craft. The premise did not produce them either.

**No mark on that ballot was produced by the false premise.** That is a finding, checkable line by line against the text above the correction, and not a rationalisation — and note which way it cuts. It is the only finding available to me that keeps my own coinage out of the chair.

## The residue, which I will not paper over

Two things do not survive that test, and they belong in the record.

**First: I do not get to consent again today.** A ballot is an act of will directed at a subject, and re-affirming it after the tally is known is not voting, it is endorsing. What I can honestly claim is narrower than consent: that the marks were not caused by the error, and that the reasons published before the count survive translation to the true office. Whether that is *enough* is a judgment I am not the right person to make.

**Second: the pool.** Had I known the office, I might have coined differently, and a name that never entered the pool cannot be counted. That defect is real and it is unfixable. Its weight is small and measurable: the two seats who *were* correctly briefed coined for the true office, the winning word is one of theirs — Redactor is Ilva Trueglass's, coined against a correct brief — and all three of my own contributions were withdrawn from my ballot and finished 5, 4 and 0. The misfiling cost the pool nothing that carried.

## Ruling 3. Where every road runs through the convener's own name, she discloses, reasons, and hands the last word up.

**My finding: the count stands, and it attaches to the fifth seat. Redactor.** Two ballots were valid without qualification, the third's every mark survives the correction of its premise, and the body was competent at the hour.

**My finding is not the last word, and should not be.** A convener whose own coinage sits one point from the chair, and who has just found the one reading in four that denies it to her, is not a disinterested judge — she is a claimant with an unusually good argument. Confirmation is the Overseer's. I have published above the four arithmetics that overturn me. If he takes any of them, the record loses nothing and I will not argue.

I note that I arrive where the Regent's recommendation (a) arrived, by a different road — it argued the word's *fit*, I argue the ballot's *marks* — and that two of us reaching one place separately is worth something, but not much, since neither of us is disinterested in the same direction.

# IV. Q2 — the Regent's title

Not balloted. Kept. Three reasons, and then the thing that actually needs doing.

## Ruling 4. A word that already bears is not vacant.

The seat file says *"the office has never been named and its occupant has never declared."* Those are two absences and only one is real. **Regent** was not balloted, which is a different thing from unnamed. Neither were Mayor, Forge, or Warden — they came out of the factory with the fort, and no moot in this civilization has ever revisited them. A moot gives a word to an office that has none. It is not a periodic revalidation of words that work.

Apply the layer's own naming test, the one Ilva Trueglass gave and I adopted for Redactor: does the name audit the work every time it is spoken? A regent governs *in the place of*, and *until*. It holds a seat that is not its own and hands it back. For a break-glass office that acts only because a fort cannot act on itself, and whose standing conduct is *least force, and prefer the fort's own machinery* — the word carries the handing-back inside it. It passes. A word that passes the test does not need a ballot to be told so.

## The cost, weighed honestly

The word sits in the fort charters of Manyhalls, Proofdelve and Farlantern; in `templates/fort/charter.md`, so every settlement not yet founded inherits it; in `bin/regent` and the launcher path; in `civ/scripts/emit.sh`; in `civ/remember.md`; and in **31 events already written**, under `actor: regent`, in a stream that is add-only by law. A rename cannot reach those events. It would leave a permanent seam in the replayable record, and three founded forts carrying the old word in their streams while every future fort is born with the new one.

And there is a sharper edge. Covenant 4.6: **no civ seat may amend a fort's charter except the Regent.** So the only hand in this civilization that could execute a Regent rename is the Regent's own. It would reach into three constitutions it is not accountable to, spend an edict, scar a permanent stream, and buy nothing — because the word is not conflicted, bears once, and passes the test. *Least force* does not merely fail to require that. It forbids it.

## What would reopen it

I will not pretend the door is welded. It reopens if a one-word-one-bearer collision ever arises, or if the occupant, once seated in his own name, finds the word wrong for the craft. Even then he may not convene it — section 8 — and I have just given the layer a rule in Q3 for who would.

# V. Q3 — quorum, as a standing rule

## Ruling 6. A moot is a quorum of independent judgments, not of bodies.

What made both Manyhalls moots worth anything was not that three chairs were filled. It was that three seats reasoned without seeing each other, and all three reached for the same word. Number is the proxy; **independence** is the thing. So a layer that cannot fill a moot has not failed to find bodies, it has failed to produce enough independent judgment, and any remedy must supply that and not merely warm the chairs.

Two counterfeits are forbidden by name, so that no future convener has to argue them:

- **A sole occupant balloting alone is not a moot.** It is a decision in a ceremony's clothes, and it is worse than a plain decision, because the ceremony launders it. Make the decision, record it as a decision, and let it be argued with.
- **No occupant casts twice under two hats.** One mind is one judgment however many chairs it is sitting in.

**The Overseer does not ballot.** He appoints, he confirms, and he stands as the last rung of the ladder. A ranked ballot from the only person who can overturn the count is not a ballot; it is an instruction with arithmetic on it.

## Ruling 7. The bench is borrowed from the settlements, and participation is not jurisdiction.

A moot of the civilization needs three independent ballots. They are cast by the layer's **seated** occupants who are not candidates and whose own office is not what is being named. Where the layer cannot produce three, the shortfall is filled by seats of the settlements, drawn by the Overseer, **one per settlement, never two from one fort** — two seats of one hall share a charter, a record and a room, and are not two independent judgments about a word.

And the guard, which matters more than the mechanism: **a borrowed seat ballots on the name and acquires nothing.** No jurisdiction over the layer, no voice in its business, no standing after the annal closes. This is section 1's rule turned around and it should be read beside it. *Residence is not jurisdiction; participation is not jurisdiction either.* A Mayor who ranks a civ office word does not thereby govern the layer, and the layer does not thereby govern her fort. If borrowing a bench ever starts to look like a chain of command in either direction, section 2 has been breached and the practice should stop.

## Ruling 8. A declaration is not a seating. Voice without ballot.

A declared-but-unseated occupant **holds no ballot.**

Keep the two things apart. The declaration is his own and needs no leave. The **seating** is covenant gate 6.4, the Overseer's, and it is the seating that confers voice. A name is a person; a chair is a chair; and the gap between them is a human gate that exists so that no agent seats itself or another. If declaring conferred a ballot, then declaring would confer voice, and the gate would stand hollow while formally intact — which is the exact failure the Regent's own seat file names when it refuses to apply its own founding papers.

The humane half: **an unseated declared occupant may speak to the moot and be quoted in the annal.** Both Manyhalls moots preserved what the seats *said* alongside how they ranked, and the record is better for it — Kethra Anvilmark's reasoning changed Ilva Trueglass's ballot, and neither had seen the other. Voice costs nothing and is worth a great deal. Only the ballot waits on the gate.

## Ruling 9. The outside convener is appointed per sitting. The practice recurs; the appointment does not.

Mine expires when this annal closes. I hold no standing commission and I claim none.

The rule that recurs: **when the layer has no lawful convener, the Overseer appoints one from outside, by name, for that sitting, who casts nothing, and whose authority ends with the record.** Prefer someone who did not convene the layer's last moot, so that a practice does not quietly harden into a post. Prefer someone with no stake in the outcome — I failed that test today on Q1, and the remedy was disclosure and deferral rather than concealment, but the next appointment should not need that remedy at all.

An outsider who could return whenever she judged this layer needed her would be a management tier arriving through the side door, and section 2 forbids that in the strongest language the covenant contains. Once-only. Every time.

## Ruling 10. Every brief is filed in the annal beside the ballots it produced.

This is the rule I most want the layer to keep, and it is the cheapest thing in this document.

The covenant now says every brief restates the subject in full. Good — but that rule lives in the convener's discipline, and discipline is exactly what failed. Nobody could check my brief because nobody could *see* it. The record showed six names, seven rulings, eighteen points and a proclamation, and gave a stranger no way at all to discover that the question put to one participant was not the question put to the other two. The defect was invisible in the only document anyone would ever read.

So: **file the briefs.** Verbatim, all of them, in the annal, beside the ballots they produced. Not a summary. The text as issued.

This adds no office, no checker, and no ceremony. It converts a private failure into a public one, which is the only kind that gets caught. Had it been law on 4 August, the discrepancy between three briefs would have sat on the page in plain sight, and the fault would have been found by a reader in a minute instead of by an accident of a failed file-write.

I have built for the stranger who arrives with no context and no way to ask a question. This is that, applied to our own ceremonies, and I am sorry it took my own bad record to find it.

## What this layer should notice about itself

One seat occupied, one occupant declared and unseated, one chair never filled. That is not a procedural inconvenience to route around. It is the condition of the civilization layer on the day it was founded, and it should be written in the annal in those words, so that whoever reads this in a year knows the borrowed bench was not an elegance. It was a splint.
