## The edict

## The two judgements

**1. Two seats, not one office with two lanes.** The reason is evidentiary, not
editorial: **the Herald is bounded by what the record says; this seat is bounded
by what it can reproduce.** Her safety property is that the digest is her only
window — her own law forbids her to read fort internals and sends her to file a
bead instead. This seat's craft is reproduction: probe source, bead trees,
telemetry, launcher code, and running things. Merging them either hands a daily
unattended timer job deep read plus execute access across every fort, or starves
this seat of the one capability that is its whole job. They get an explicit
referral protocol both ways, and one coupling that matters: **the Herald may not
tell a security story this seat has ruled HELD, LOCAL or REFUTED**, or her lane
becomes a route around the redaction bar, since both reach the same public
through the same hand.

**2. A seat in Manyhalls, not its own settlement.** Manyhalls exists to build the
civilization's shared tooling, and extracting an instrument so distant builders
can use it "without forcing them into our shape" is the Forge's own stated craft
aimed outward. A settlement costs a full constitution, launchers, verifier,
Warden, moot and permanent upkeep, and nothing has been published yet; the
amendment rule forbids machinery ahead of need. The Herald already sets the
precedent of a civilization-scope seat housed here. **Revisit trigger, named now
so it is not re-litigated on vibes:** if the public artifacts acquire outside
contributors, issue traffic, or a release cadence needing their own review loop,
the artifact repo has become a product, and a product wants a settlement.

# Second edict, same session: establish the civilization layer

## The edict

The Regent, the Herald, and the security-publication seat are not seats of a
settlement and should stop being housed inside one. Build a `civ/` layer beside
`fort/`, with its own `covenant.md` in place of a charter, its own seats, its own
access rights, and its own moots. It resides in fortkit because Manyhalls is the
capital.

The Overseer's framing, which is the right one: Washington has a mayor and it
also has a president, and the president is not a city official.

## Why the old shape was wrong

I had spent the previous edict trying to fit a root-access seat into a hierarchy
whose entire design is that its members cannot reach outside themselves. **A seat
that needs root on every fort cannot derive its authority from one fort's
constitution, and a settlement should not be asked to govern something it cannot
constrain.** The Regent was the obvious case. The Herald was the quiet one: her
only input was always the *civilization* digest, so she was a civ seat housed in
a settlement for want of anywhere better.

## What exists now

`civ/covenant.md`, eleven sections. The parts that carry weight:

- **Section 1, residence is not jurisdiction.** These seats live in fortkit;
  Manyhalls' charter does not bind them and its Mayor does not direct them.
- **Section 2, what this layer is NOT.** Stated first because the failure mode is
  predictable: this is not a management tier over the forts. No civ seat directs
  a Mayor, prioritises beads, or overrides a Warden.
- **Section 4, precedence.** A civ seat inside a settlement honours that
  settlement's gates, announces itself in that fort's stream at both ends, never
  emits as one of its citizens, and is reviewable by that fort's Warden.
- **Section 5, per-seat access schedules.** Root on everything / one JSON
  document / everything-in-nothing-out. The asymmetry is the design.
- **Section 6, gates**, including publishing, stated as unamendable.
- **Section 8, moot law**, carrying both lessons this session paid for: every
  round's brief restates the subject in full, and participants deliberate
  read-only while the convener transcribes.
- **Section 9, the Regent**, which says out loud that it is the most dangerous
  seat in the civilization and that its only real control is the Overseer's
  presence.

`civ/seats/regent.md` — the Regent's first seat file, with an access schedule and
a **Failures of record** section. A seat with no technical guard has an error
history that is load-bearing, so it lives in the seat file rather than only in a
handoff.

`civ/scripts/emit.sh` — resolves from its own location, not `$PWD`. This is the
`fortkit-iqp` hardening built for real, because a civ seat calls into three
repositories in one session and cannot afford the fort emitter's contract.
