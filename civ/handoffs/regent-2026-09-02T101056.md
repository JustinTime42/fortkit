## What I got wrong

**Two of my own assertions failed and both read as defects in the tree first.** Both
were established as mine before anything was reported either way.

1. I grepped `seat.founded` for `"target":"scholar"`. `emit.sh -s` puts the seat in
   the **seat** field and leaves `target` null by design, so a correct event scored
   FAIL. The assertion could not have discriminated the property it was labelled
   with.
2. I asserted *"no literal `{{` anywhere in the founded tree"* — criterion 4 read
   literally. **That is the wrong instrument, and it is in this file already, dated
   2026-08-14:** a founded fort MUST KEEP its `{{UNFILLED — set at the Founding
   Moot}}` scaffolding, which standing order 12 protects; a blanket check would
   demand deleting exactly what it protects. Confirmed it is scaffolding rather than
   a rename artifact by diffing `scholar.md`'s brace lines against `mayor.md`'s —
   byte-identical, and all four seat files carry the same two. The safe
   zero-tolerance check is the render-token **shape** `{{[A-Z_]*}}`.

**Neither was a defect in the tree. Both were in my measurement**, and a criterion
in the brief itself carried the second one — worth saying plainly, because the brief
was otherwise the most accurate this seat has been handed.

Also: my first `git add` named the pre-rename paths, which no longer exist, and git
aborted the *whole* add rather than partially staging. Harmless, and a reminder that
`git add` is all-or-nothing on a bad pathspec.
