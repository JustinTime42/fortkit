## Item 1 — `fortkit-rw86`, the blocking finding

`status.sh:42` was `git log -g -1 --format=%cd`. **Under `git log -g`, `%cd` is
still the COMMIT's committer date**; the reflog entry's own time is `%gd`. The
`-g` is inert for that value, so the previous sitting's repair read the same
quantity the defect did and merely relabelled it.

Now `%gd`, with git's `origin/main@{...}` wrapper stripped by `sed`, under a
seven-line comment ending "do not simplify this back" — because the defect is
invisible on inspection: the `-g` *looks* like it makes `%cd` a reflog quantity.

**Positive control, which is the whole evidence.** A green proves nothing unless
old and new differ in the tree tested:

| fort | OLD `-g %cd` | NEW `-g %gd` | **no `-g` `%cd`** |
|---|---|---|---|
| fortkit | 22:17:58 | 22:18:05 | 22:17:58 ← identical to OLD |
| ForgeOs | 22:15:50 | 22:15:55 | 22:15:50 ← identical to OLD |
| longburn | 22:16:06 | 22:16:11 | 22:16:06 ← identical to OLD |

The third column is the discriminating measurement: **dropping `-g` changes
nothing about the old value.** That is the finding stated as an experiment rather
than as an argument, and it is what I would want a reviewer to re-run.

**Fresh-clone degradation, verified rather than reasoned about.** A local clone's
`.git/logs/refs/remotes/origin/` holds `HEAD` and no `main`, so *both* commands
return empty, `${SYNCED:+ …}` omits the clause, `rev-list` still exits 0, and the
line prints with no synced clause and no error. Unchanged by the repair.
