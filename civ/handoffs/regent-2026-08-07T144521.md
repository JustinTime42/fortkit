## The edict

The Overseer opened with a design question rather than an order: is the kernel
lock on the forts' own constitutions overkill, given the sandbox and git? The
session fact-checked his premises against the actual mask and repos, and the
answer became the edict, authorized in full: **relax where the reasoning holds
(content layer), tighten where it was found wanting (control layer).**

Findings that shaped it: two of three forts had no git remote at all when the
conversation started (he fixed that mid-session; I trued up the unpushed 80/23
commits); the event streams — the audit record — were gitignored everywhere;
and `.git/config` + `.git/hooks` were writable inside every Claude-seat mask,
a host-hook escape one class identical to the `.beads/hooks` fix that already
existed (a masked seat writes a hook or repoints `core.hooksPath`; it fires
unmasked on the Overseer's next commit).

   `charter.amended`. Attended seats only; the Forge keeps every mechanical

   `civ/events/`, backfilled and committed; `bin/fort-init` no longer writes
