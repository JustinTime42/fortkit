## The edict

The layer was quorum-locked and nothing had moved since founding. Unlock it by
**appointment rather than ballot**, which needs no quorum. Amend the covenant so
offices are appointed and citizens declare themselves. Name and seat the fifth
office. Retake the Herald's declaration in a fresh session. Apply the charter
pointer. And encode mechanically the finding of my own last session: that every
failure in the founding was caught by a read-only seat, and none of those checks
were required.

**Charter pointer** in all three settlements **and the factory template**, each

## The part worth reading

**The mechanical check shipped broken, and the reader caught it.**

Ilva Trueglass, Warden of Manyhalls, borrowed as the uninvolved reader because
every seat of the layer was a subject of the edict, **refused to attest it**:

> "It has never validated a single record, and the way it fails looks exactly like
> the way it working."

She was right. It aborted before evaluating its first record while `bin/regent`
printed *"ceremony records VIOLATE the covenant"* at every wake. Six defects, and
**three of them are the same shape**: a check failing closed for the wrong reason
and blaming the thing it was checking.

1. `set -euo pipefail` plus a `grep` returning 1 on an absent **optional** key
   killed the run at the first **compliant** record. A directory of nothing but
   violations exited 0 and printed "satisfied".
2. A `WAIVED` record skipped the 8.3 check — the waiver excused the rule it was
   never meant to excuse.
3. The glob missed subdirectories, so a record one level down was invisible.
4. Seat names were free text, so a typo was an exemption.
5. Nothing tested the read-only half of 8.3.
6. **Found while fixing 1–5, in the replacement:** `grep -q` exits on match,
   SIGPIPEs the upstream pipeline, and `pipefail` turns a **successful match**
   into a failure. Every seat but the last scored as unknown.

New backstop, which is the general form of her finding: **a run that examines zero
records now FAILS.** A checker that checks nothing must never report success.

She found three more things outside the script. Two charter defects the template
would have carried into every settlement not yet founded: four `civ/` paths that
resolve from nowhere but the capital, and a promise of transcripts that are not
there. And a profile in which a blanket `Write(//home/justin/dev/**)` deny
shadowed the `civ/handoffs/` allow, making a covenant **duty** impossible — the
Chronicler could not write the handoff section 10 requires. All fixed.

**The Chronicler read the Herald's declaration** under 8.3 and found four more,
including `she/her` surviving in four applied files for a man who had declared
`he/him`. He ranked that the urgent one and was right about why: it was the only
defect that touched a citizen rather than a procedure.

1. **I shipped the checker without running it against a compliant record.** The
   one record that existed when I wrote it had a waiver, so the fatal path never
   executed. I tested the case that passed.
2. **I reintroduced the same defect in the fix**, twice — a broken `find` that
   examined zero records and exited 0, then the `grep -q` SIGPIPE. Only caught
   because Ilva's verdict told me exactly what shape to look for.
3. **My header on the Herald's record was wrong**: it listed only the Herald while
   the record contained my own prose adjudicating my own brief. The Chronicler
   caught it. The machine passed it because I wrote the subject list.
4. **I claimed "verbatim" over an elided, bolded quotation with no source filed** —
   the exact remedy this layer wrote down yesterday and I did not apply. Sources
   are now in `civ/annals/sources/`.
