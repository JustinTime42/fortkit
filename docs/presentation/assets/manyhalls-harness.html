Four seats, separated on purpose

Each seat holds the power its job needs and nothing more. The reason for the split is the whole point: the seat that reads the world is never the seat that can touch it, and the seat that writes code is never the seat that reviews or ships it.

The two foundations

The seats above are only as trustworthy as what sits beneath them. Both layers are designed so a mistake or an injected instruction fails closed rather than open.

Enforcement — the wall

Permission rules match the text of a command, so they bind a spelling, not a file. The kernel mask binds the inode. Every spelling of a masked path, including ones nobody has thought of, reads empty.

  • bwrap masks per seat — each runtime sees only what its job needs
  • gate 1 the fort's own constitution — Warden + me
  • gate 2 .env and secrets — denied to every agent
  • gate 3 anything public-facing — mine alone

Memory — what survives the session

A new conversation starts without losing what matters. Facts carry their provenance, stale ones are superseded rather than deleted, and nothing in the record is ever quietly rewritten.

  • fact ledger — tiered, sourced, admission-controlled
  • current.md — the distilled view, regenerated not edited
  • handoffs — one session briefs the next
  • events — append-only JSONL, the replayable history
Regent

Some work the fort is deliberately unable to do to itself — repair a launcher, change its own enforcement layer, carry law between forts. The Regent does that, from outside: a civilization-level seat that runs unmasked, invoked by hand and only while I'm present. It is never scheduled, and it announces every edict into the fort's own event stream.

The loop between me and it

The design goal is to write myself out of the primary loop. I stay in flow; the harness works asynchronously on whatever is ready, and reaches for me only at the edges.

Where I touch it

  1. Intent in. Specs, decisions, and the occasional unblock. The Mayor turns them into work.
  2. Gates. A bead that needs me is labelled the moment it does — gate-1, gate-2, gate-3 — never buried.
  3. Push and deploy. I approve every one, and I'm told exactly what and why.

Where it runs without me

  1. Decompose and claim. The Mayor files the bead tree; the Forge claims whatever is ready.
  2. Build, verify, review. Code is written under a mask, checked by the verifier, then judged by the Warden.
  3. Report back. A digest surfaces the decisions at quiescence, so they reach me without my reading scrollback.
