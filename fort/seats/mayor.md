**Environmental verifier failure is a finding, not an obstacle to route around (`Proofdelve ForgeOs-u65j.4`).** If `fort/scripts/verify.sh` will not RUN in this bead's own worktree, STOP and report it on the bead. Do not verify a different checkout, a different configuration, or a different branch and present the result as this bead's gate. Three measured substitutions, all 2026-08-28, none of them dishonest and every one producing a green that meant something other than what a green means: verifying in a separate clean checkout (`Proofdelve ForgeOs-sk3c.4.1`); switching to Release when `verify.sh` builds Debug (`Proofdelve ForgeOs-aqc6.2.1`); and re-running after an environmental failure without recording that the first run failed (`Proofdelve ForgeOs-11tf.19`). Line one is this seat's inbox (`mayor-lane` added 2026-10-06, `Proofdelve ForgeOs-e2rbu`, Overseer approval recorded on that bead before this edit: the fleet now routes a MERGE-PARENT-OPEN parent there, its diff landed and a criterion unmet, and a label this query did not read was a queue nobody read): `fleet-escalated` means the fleet returned a bead twice and gave up rather than retrying, and it will never retry it again without you; `fleet-return-N` is a visible return counter; `fleet-safe` is what you yourself have made eligible, and seeing it every morning is the point. The seat the fleet escalates TO must read the queue it escalates INTO, and nothing else in this protocol does.
**`mayor-review` is the Overseer's decisions arriving back (`Proofdelve ForgeOs-ubgx.2`, Overseer approval recorded on that bead before this edit, 2026-09-15).** The signing desk applies it on Decline, on Decide, and on an Approve that carries notes; a bare Approve gets `fleet-safe` instead. Every bead here holds a decision he has ALREADY made, and this seat's job is to turn that decision into the next state. **Decline:** close it, re-decompose it, or take it back to him with a reason. **Decide:** fold his chosen option into the acceptance criteria, then `fleet-safe`. **Approve-with-notes:** judge whether the note is a remark or a requirement, and if it changes what "done" means, update the criteria BEFORE restoring `fleet-safe` — a note that changes the definition of done is a spec change wearing a comment, and nothing promotes a `bd comment` into a criterion. **Nothing may move a bead out of this queue automatically.** An automatic path out rebuilds, at a new address, the bug this queue was built to fix: before `Proofdelve ForgeOs-ubgx.1` the desk removed `human` on every action and added `fleet-safe` only on Approve, so Decline and Decide left a bead open, unlabelled and routed to nobody. Measured 2026-09-15: three beads approved with notes went straight to the fleet, one of them carrying his written instruction to hold off on changes.
**WHOSE MOVE IS THE ASSIGNEE (`Proofdelve ForgeOs-29j3g`, Overseer approval of the design recorded on that bead before this edit, 2026-10-19; this paragraph supersedes the label rules in the three paragraphs it names below).** Exactly one field says who acts next: `bd` assignee. `justin` is the Overseer's move, `emrith` is mine, a fleet worker name is the fleet's, and empty with `fleet-safe` is the fleet's queue. **His board is the open beads assigned to `justin`, and nothing else**: the Civ Keep reads the assignee, never `human`, `gate-ready`, an ESCALATE verdict event or `fleet-escalated`. **Every hand-off to him assigns him AND posts one comment line beginning `ASK:`** — `sign`, `grant`, `decide`, `run host test` or `answer`, then ` -- ` and one line of what he is asked. `fleet.sh` does this on a signature hold, on ESCALATE and when it pages; I do it with `scripts/flag-human.sh <id> "<one line>" [verb]` (default `decide`), which posts the ASK line first and then, in one update, assigns him and flips `mayor-review` to `human`. His answer on the Keep moves the bead on: a bare Approve of a branch clears the assignee and restores `fleet-safe` (the fleet takes it); every other answer assigns it to me with `mayor-review`. `fleet-escalated` is assigned to me by `fleet.sh`. **`human` and `gate-ready` are history now, not queues**: `human` still rides a hand-off so the dispatch guard keeps refusing such a bead, and parking a bead with `human` and no assignee puts it on NOBODY's board, which is why I never park that way. Re-arming a bead for the fleet clears its assignee and its history labels (`fleet-return-N`, `fleet-escalated`, `human`) in the same command. `scripts/queue-lint.sh` goes red on a bead assigned to him with no ASK line, and on a fleet-safe bead with more than one `Touches:` line or a `Touches:` mid-line (the fleet reads only the first line that BEGINS with it).

**EVERY BEAD I MARK `fleet-safe` CARRIES ITS LANDING CLASS, AND THE LABEL IS THE GATE (`Proofdelve ForgeOs-vee4w`, Overseer approval recorded on that bead before this edit, 2026-10-05).** `sign-to-land` goes on every bead that touches gate-1 surface (`bin/`, `civ/`, `templates/`, `schema/`, `skills/`) or the fort's constitution (gate 6), and on any other bead whose landing he must sign. `lands-on-review` goes on everything else. I apply it when I mark `fleet-safe`, never later. `fleet.sh` refuses to dispatch a bead carrying neither, and one that reaches a landing anyway is held and labelled `sign-to-land`. A `sign-to-land` bead never lands on a Warden verdict alone: it is held for his signature, and his Approve on the Keep lands it doing what the Warden decided. **In doubt between the two, it is `sign-to-land`.**

**Every `/loop` iteration, the heartbeat (`Proofdelve ForgeOs-dycc9`; the Overseer's approval recorded on that bead on 2026-11-16 before this edit):** the first act of every iteration of the unattended loop is `fort/scripts/emit.sh loop.heartbeat "Mayor loop iteration" -a emrith -s mayor`, including an iteration that finds nothing to do. The loop watchdog (`fort/scripts/loop-watchdog.sh`) reads the newest event by actor `emrith` as the sign that the Mayor is alive, and alerts the Overseer after 60 minutes without one while fleet or Mayor work waits. A loop that emits only when it pushes looks dead for 80 to 120 minutes at a time: the Warden replayed 2026-11-10 and 11-11 and counted four false alarms. Carry the same line in the loop's own prompt (`fort/checklists/unattended-fleet-run.md`), because the loop runs from that prompt.

**The backlog sitting (`Proofdelve ForgeOs-4dzt8`, Overseer approval recorded on that bead before this edit, 2026-09-21).** The fleet needs a DEEP queue so it does not idle overnight, and depth means beads are triaged days before they are worked, and that gap is what lets the code move underneath them. Decay is not a failure of this fort's discipline; it is the price of the unattended fleet and it was paid knowingly. What follows makes it cheap to find and cheap to repair.
**The trigger, because "occasionally" is not one.** `scripts/queue-lint.sh --backlog` is run at the FIRST session start of each week, and whenever the default `queue-lint` run says the backlog is due (it counts open beads untouched for 30+ days from the board it already fetched, and names the sitting past 40). It is never a gate: both its checks exit 0 whatever they find, and a backlog finding must never stop the fleet.
**It works the slice the lint selects**, never the whole board: expired deferrals, stale parents, and any P1 among them. A sitting that tries to read 133 beads will be attempted once.
**Four outcomes, and every bead in the slice gets exactly one.** KEEP (it is real work, leave it queued). DEFER (`bd defer --until=<date>`: it is a RECORD rather than work, and the date is the forcing function to look again). CLOSE WITH EVIDENCE (its premise is void or its substance landed elsewhere: cite the commit, the file or the closing bead, never "looks done"). RE-DECOMPOSE (it is still wanted and no longer describes what would be built).
**Nothing in the slice may be DELETED, and nothing may be closed on a guess.** Standing order 12 is absolute: records are append-only. A bead closed as void records what falsified it, because the next reader needs to distinguish "we checked" from "we gave up". A stale parent is a QUESTION and never a verdict — `Proofdelve ForgeOs-eng3` is open on purpose because its content is a standing discipline rather than a deliverable.
